# Visual and UX design

## Experience idea: one connected workspace

The site should feel like a calm, precise creative instrument rather than a noisy AI marketplace. The recurring visual motif is a fine mint route connecting distinct modes of work: a conversation becomes an image direction, a video sequence and finally a published website. This makes the unified platform tangible without inventing live product behavior.

## Art direction

- Pure white canvas, warm off-white section alternation and hairline cool-gray borders.
- Near-black Poppins display type with restrained Inter body copy.
- Mint is the action/focus color; teal communicates connection; cyan, blue and purple identify individual products.
- Editorial asymmetry, ruled labels, oversized but controlled headings and deliberate negative space.
- Product visuals are purpose-built semantic interface illustrations, not photographs of nonexistent software and not presented as customer results.
- Motion is limited to small entrance/transform feedback, with reduced-motion fallbacks and no blocking scroll effects.

## Homepage narrative

1. **Promise:** “Everything AI. One Platform.” with immediate product scope and two clear routes.
2. **Ecosystem visual:** four connected product panels around one shared workspace spine; mobile becomes a legible vertical flow.
3. **Four product chapters:** Chat uses a conversational composition, Image Studio a contact-sheet/artboard, Video Studio a timeline and Websites a responsive page wireframe. Layouts and accents differ while typography remains coherent.
4. **Model choice:** explain model selection without claiming an unverified catalog.
5. **Why Orindle:** fewer disconnected handoffs, shared context and transparent usage; no unsupported productivity percentages.
6. **Connected workflow:** a concrete campaign example from brief to site.
7. **Use cases:** role-based jobs-to-be-done, not fake customer stories.
8. **Credits and pricing:** one-time packs, shared wallet and variable usage cost stated plainly.
9. **FAQ and final CTA:** answer purchase objections and route to account stage or pricing.

## Persuasion and trust

Use concrete explanations, visible prices and plain next steps. Avoid countdowns, “last chance,” invented ratings, logos, testimonials, savings claims and hidden recurring terms. Primary CTA language stays consistent. Preview-only interactions explain their status before submission.

## Content design

Articles open with a direct answer, include a contents nav when long, use descriptive H2/H3 sections, actionable examples and selected authoritative external links. Tables have responsive treatment and meaningful headers. Inline product links appear where useful, not after every paragraph.

## Footer and navigation

Desktop navigation is compact with a keyboard-accessible Products menu. Mobile uses a labelled button, focus-managed disclosure and large touch targets. The footer includes all products, company/help routes, legal/credits routes and a concise platform description. No dead social icons are added without real destinations.

## Authentication experience

Prompt 2 extends the same visual language into login, signup, recovery, reset, verification and callback presentations. A shared bordered white workspace sits on the off-white canvas; a charcoal desktop aside explains the one-account/four-product model with original brand geometry and real HTML labels. Mobile removes the aside and prioritizes the form. There is no separate auth theme or decorative stock imagery.

Trust comes from clarity: persistent labels, visible requirements, honest frontend-preview notices, respectful recovery options and no artificial urgency. Google/GitHub are presented as planned alternatives without implying provider contact. Errors explain a next step without confirming account existence. Password guidance favors a 15+ character passphrase, password managers and paste rather than arbitrary complexity.

Detailed component, responsive and interaction decisions are in `authentication-design.md`; route/state copy is in `authentication-flows.md`.

## Customer application experience

Prompt 3 translates the editorial system into a calm, high-density creative workspace rather than a generic admin template. A slim persistent sidebar organizes only Main, Workspace and Account. The top bar is quiet and contextual, with breadcrumbs, a fixture-labelled universal credit pill, notifications and a demo profile menu. A concise demo notice replaces marketing chrome and prevents fixture data being mistaken for a real account.

The overview prioritizes the next useful action: greeting and balance, four distinct product entries, suggested creation, recent projects/generations and a compact usage summary. It avoids decorative analytics and oversized charts. Product color appears in small icon wells, borders and selection cues rather than full-page gradients.

Chat centers a substantial composer and readable message measure. Image Studio uses a configuration rail beside a generous artboard/contact sheet. Video Studio separates setup from queued/processing/completed/failed jobs. Websites uses an actual responsive HTML preview, page/section rail and contextual AI/code panel. Wallet and history emphasize transparent status and one-time pricing. Empty states use short guidance and a real next action.

Fixture imagery is clearly labelled and meaningful interface copy remains HTML. Dialogs are reserved for focused preview/detail/destructive confirmation. Motion is limited to brief state transitions; static progress never pretends to advance. See `dashboard-architecture.md` and `dashboard-navigation.md` for implementation boundaries.
