# Blog content strategy

## Cluster thesis

Help a creator or small team move from an idea to a finished digital presence using the right AI tool at each step. Each article answers a separate intent and links to the next useful step; the cluster must not become six reworded product advertisements.

## Research protocol

For each article, inspect current official sources and representative high-ranking/competing educational material where accessible. Record query variations, recurring questions and content gaps before drafting. Model capability statements use provider documentation and avoid unverified benchmark claims. Technical/how-to claims use primary sources where possible. Every external link adds context or evidence; no link is inserted purely for SEO.

## Editorial standards

- Useful introduction with a direct answer, not a long definition of AI.
- Original examples with concrete inputs, decisions and review steps.
- H2/H3 hierarchy, scannable lists and a TOC for substantial guides.
- Clear limitations, human review and rights/safety considerations.
- No invented performance numbers, customer stories, prompt output or universal “best” claims.
- No keyword stuffing or mandatory word count; depth follows intent.
- A single natural Orindle CTA near the conclusion plus contextual links where helpful.

## Research log

Research performed **2026-11-05**. Search-result pages were used to understand intent and competing coverage, not as evidence for unverified model rankings or prices.

### 1. Best AI tools for creators

- **Intent/questions:** best tools by creator type, what to use for writing/images/video/websites, how to avoid overlapping subscriptions, whether one platform can replace a stack, how to evaluate cost and rights.
- **Competing coverage:** listicles from Rework, Storyflow, Zapier-style tool roundups and smaller affiliate sites emphasize ranked tools, feature tables and monthly prices. Many claims change quickly and few explain handoffs or evaluation.
- **Gap selected:** a vendor-neutral category/selection framework, a complete brief-to-site workflow, specialist-versus-connected-platform decision criteria and responsible review. No universal “best” ranking or unverified prices.
- **Primary/context sources:** https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide and https://airc.nist.gov/ for people-first publishing and risk review.

### 2. GPT vs Claude vs Gemini

- **Intent/questions:** which is best for writing, coding, long documents, multimodal input, cost and everyday use; current versions; benchmark winners; whether multiple models are necessary.
- **Competing coverage:** current results are dominated by exact version, benchmark and “winner” tables. They frequently disagree and mix consumer-product features with model capability, making them brittle.
- **Gap selected:** distinguish model families from products, avoid unsupported winners, compare evaluation criteria and give readers a repeatable task-specific test set/rubric.
- **Primary sources:** OpenAI model catalog https://platform.openai.com/docs/models; Anthropic model overview https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/models/overview; Gemini models https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models; prompting guidance https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/prompting-strategies and https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/system-prompts. Exact version claims are deliberately omitted from the article.

### 3. AI image generation

- **Intent/questions:** prompt formulas, styles, photorealism, composition, aspect ratios, negative prompts, consistency, fixing artifacts and prompt examples.
- **Competing coverage:** prompt lists and six-part formulas are common; many encourage keyword stuffing, camera-name accumulation or style imitation without discussing the communication goal and iteration record.
- **Gap selected:** purpose-first art direction, one-variable iteration, a worked product-image brief, practical troubleshooting and text/rights review.
- **Primary sources:** OpenAI image generation/editing guide https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/image-generation; Google Cloud image prompt guidance https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/image/img-gen-prompt-guide (feature support is model-specific and must be checked).

### 4. AI video from text and images

- **Intent/questions:** text-to-video versus image-to-video, prompt structure, camera movement, reference images, duration, continuity, audio/captions and common failures.
- **Competing coverage:** tool rankings and generic Subject+Action+Camera formulas dominate; fewer articles cover shot planning, continuity review and final editing responsibilities.
- **Gap selected:** a three-shot worked sequence, separate text/image starting-point decisions, motion-specific troubleshooting and delivery review.
- **Primary sources:** Google Cloud text-to-video https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/generate-videos-from-text, image-to-video https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/generate-videos-from-an-image and first/last-frame workflow https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/generate-videos-from-first-and-last-frames. These sources establish that workflows exist, not that every model supports every control.

### 5. AI website without coding

- **Intent/questions:** how to prompt a builder, required content/pages, customization, domain/forms, mobile, SEO and publishing checklist.
- **Competing coverage:** builder-specific tutorials emphasize speed and “five-minute” claims; some recommend placeholder social proof or thin SEO pages.
- **Gap selected:** no fabricated proof, real business content, responsive/accessibility checks, working-form verification and people-first SEO rather than guaranteed rankings.
- **Primary sources:** Google SEO Starter Guide above; W3C WCAG 2.2 https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/; W3C images tutorial https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/; web.dev Web Vitals https://web.dev/articles/vitals.

### 6. AI for small business

- **Intent/questions:** practical use cases, content calendars, videos without a studio, websites, budget, tool choices, quality control and ROI.
- **Competing coverage:** many pages focus only on video volume, tool promotion or unsupported savings/earnings. Few connect one campaign across copy, image, video and web with clear review ownership.
- **Gap selected:** one fictional workshop campaign, a realistic weekly operating rhythm, measurable business actions, credit-budget questions and responsible claims/privacy checks.
- **Primary sources:** NIST AI Resource Center https://airc.nist.gov/ and Generative AI Profile resources https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/ai-risk-management-framework-resources; FTC AI claims guidance https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/02/keep-your-ai-claims-check; Google Search Essentials https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials.

Published: 2026-11-05. All six planned articles are implemented and interlinked. Review the date-sensitive model guide at least quarterly and whenever supported provider catalogs change.

## Measurement

After a real launch, evaluate search query alignment, engagement with practical sections and assisted navigation to relevant feature pages. Do not promise first-page rankings. Refresh content based on observed questions and product changes rather than rewriting solely for keywords.
